Research

Regularised learning of dependence.

My methodological work asks how unknown spatial and network dependence can be estimated reliably in high-dimensional statistical models.

Core research question

Many statistical models require a dependence structure to be specified before estimation. When that structure is unknown, geographic adjacency, distance, sector membership or other heuristics may impose assumptions that are not supported by the data.

My work develops regularised approaches for estimating sparse dependence matrices directly from observations, while addressing identifiability, stability, model selection and predictive performance.

Methods

LASSOAdaptive LASSOPenalised QMLEMonte CarloCross-validationForecast validationNetwork learning

Application domains

Environmental statistics

Hourly particulate-matter concentrations in Bavaria, Germany, provide a setting for learning spatial and temporal autoregressive effects rather than relying solely on predefined spatial structures.

Financial networks

UK equity-market data are used to learn volatility dependence networks through penalised spatiotemporal autoregressive conditional heteroscedasticity models.

Epidemiological modelling

Malaria surveillance data motivate an endemic–epidemic framework in which epidemiological connectivity is learned from disease observations rather than imposed only through geography.
