Statistical Learning for Spatial and Spatiotemporal Dependence

I am a doctoral researcher in Statistics at the University of Glasgow. My research develops regularised methods for estimating dependence structures directly from data, with applications in environmental science, financial markets and infectious-disease epidemiology.

About

Learning dependence from data

Many statistical models assume that relationships between locations, time points or interconnected units are known in advance. My work investigates how these dependence structures can instead be estimated from observed data.

I combine spatial and spatiotemporal modelling, sparse regularisation, statistical learning and numerical optimisation, with an emphasis on reliable estimation and predictive assessment.

Research interests

What I work on

Spatial and spatiotemporal statistics

Dependence modelling across locations and time, including spatial autoregression and data-driven connectivity structures.

Statistical learning and regularisation

Sparse estimation, LASSO, penalised likelihood, model selection and out-of-sample predictive evaluation.

Computational and applied statistics

Optimisation and uncertainty assessment for environmental data, financial networks and infectious-disease surveillance.
